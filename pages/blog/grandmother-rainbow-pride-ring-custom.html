He came in with a box. Not a jewelry box. A shoebox. And inside it were probably hundreds of loose gemstones in every color you can imagine.

Most of them weren't worth much on paper. Some of them might not have even been real. But that wasn't the point. These stones belonged to his grandmother, and she had left them to him when she passed.

She was the first person in his life who fully accepted him for who he was.

The Story Behind the Stones

He grew up in a small town in Nebraska. And like a lot of people from small towns, he felt the pressure to fit in and be what everyone expected him to be. Coming out wasn't easy. But his grandmother was there for it. She didn't hesitate. She saw him completely, championed him, and made sure he knew he was loved exactly as he was.

When she passed, she left him this collection. I don't know if she imagined what they'd become. But I think she would have loved it.

He wanted to turn her stones into a rainbow pride ring. Something he could wear every day that honored her memory and celebrated who he is.

Sorting Through Hundreds of Stones

The first thing I did was sort everything by color. We needed red, orange, yellow, green, blue, and purple to complete the rainbow. That alone narrowed things down. Then, because we were doing a shared-prong band, every stone had to be close to the same size in order for the design to work structurally. So I was sorting by color, then by dimension, then by which ones had the best saturation and sparkle.

One of the constraints was that we committed to using only stones from his grandmother's collection. We weren't going to supplement with anything new. Whatever the ring was going to be, it was going to come entirely from what she left him.

The stones we ended up with aren't perfectly matched. Some are a little bigger than others. The sizes aren't identical. And honestly, I think that adds something. There's a character to the imperfection that feels right. It's not a machine-made ring. It's a handmade ring with handpicked stones from someone who loved him, and you can feel that when you look at it. That kind of intention is what I try to bring to every project, but this one carried it in a way I'll never forget.

What the Ring Became

We cast it in 14k yellow gold with the stones arranged in a full rainbow across a shared-prong band. It's bold without being loud. It makes a statement, but it's the kind of statement that invites a conversation rather than demanding attention.

And that conversation is the part that gets me. He travels the world. He wears this ring everywhere. And people stop him to ask about it. Every time, he gets to tell the story of his grandmother, what she meant to him, and who he is. The ring didn't just honor her memory. It gave him a way to carry her with him and share her with other people.

Why This Project Still Stays with Me

I connected with this one personally. I can't speak to his exact experience, but I know what it feels like to not be seen. I know what it means when someone in your family really gets you and stands in your corner when other people won't. My own grandparents played that role for me in a lot of ways.

So when he walked in with that box and told me the story, I understood immediately what this project was really about. It wasn't about the monetary value of the stones. It wasn't about clarity grades or carat weight. The value was the meaning. These were his grandmother's stones, and what they represented was worth infinitely more than any appraisal could capture. That's something I see with every heirloom piece that comes through my studio - the real value is never on the certificate.

That's the thing about custom jewelry that I think gets lost in all the talk about specs and certifications. The best pieces aren't the ones with the highest grades. They're the ones that carry something real.

Something personal. Something that can't be bought off a shelf or clicked into a cart. If you've ever wondered what the difference is between real custom and everything else, this is it.

His ring is one of the most meaningful pieces I've ever made. And it started with a shoebox.
